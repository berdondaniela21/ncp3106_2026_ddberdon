Computer Engineering
at UE

The BS Computer Engineering (BSCpE) program trains students to work on both sides of the hardware-software line — from designing circuits to writing the operating systems that run on them. It blends electrical engineering and computer science into one discipline built around embedded and computer systems.

Program Overview

What the program is about

Computer Engineering applies engineering principles to the analysis, design, and integration of hardware and software. UE's CpE curriculum is multidisciplinary by design, preparing graduates to build embedded digital systems — the microprocessor- and microcontroller-based devices found in everything from consumer electronics to industrial automation and gaming hardware. Coursework leans heavily on practical, project-based learning: students design circuits, write firmware, and build systems from the ground up across their four years in the program.

The CpE Curriculum

Built around embedded systems

The CpE curriculum and student projects focus on embedded systems — any device that uses a microprocessor or microcontroller for a specific purpose. Embedded systems show up across a wide array of household and industrial systems, including game consoles, robots, electronic toys, digital cameras, audio/video components, and even aircraft flight systems.

Freshman and sophomore courses lay a foundation in hardware, software, and the engineering sciences (math, physics, chemistry) before students move into junior and senior-level design courses. Design methodology and analysis are stressed throughout — lectures are paired with lab experiments the student designs, simulates, and analyzes before hands-on construction and testing.
